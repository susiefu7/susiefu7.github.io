# The Logbooks

There were forty-one of them, stacked in two fruit crates in the harbour office storeroom. Tomas carried them up the hill himself and would not let her help.

"They're heavy," he said.

"I'm aware of what books weigh."

"Not these ones."

* * *

She opened the first at random. Her mother's handwriting, small and upright, recorded the wind, the visibility, the passage of a trawler at dusk. Nothing remarkable. Nothing that explained why a woman would keep a log for a light that no longer shone.

It was only on the fourth evening, reading by the stove, that Iris noticed the dates. Her mother had kept the logs every night of the year Iris left. Every night but one.
